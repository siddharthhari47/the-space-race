# Models for the rocket and Space Shuttle timeline

**Bookmarked / paused:** model sourcing deferred at the user's request while the interactive widgets are completed (2026-09-26).

The timeline is at `/timeline/rocket-development.html`. Models can be added incrementally; the history already works without them. The exact mission label matters more than finding every vehicle immediately.

## Priority 1 — start here

| Model to look for | Intended exhibit | Parts that would help |

## Priority 2 — fill the historical sequence

- Goddard's 1926 **Nell**, preferably with its launch frame; the engine-above-tanks arrangement is documented in the linked NASA source.
- **A-4 / V-2**; ideally the 1942 test vehicle if a documented model exists. Otherwise label a general V-2 illustration accurately.
- **Sputnik 1 launch vehicle**, original 1957 configuration, with the satellite as a separate object if available. Send the source's exact variant name for verification.
- **Saturn V, Apollo 4**. Saturn V binary assets already exist in `public/assets/models/`; inspect whether their configuration can represent Apollo 4 before sourcing a duplicate.
- **Discovery STS-26 / STS-114**, **Atlantis STS-135**, and optionally **Challenger STS-51L / Columbia STS-107**. Mission-specific skins or documented variants of one good orbiter mesh may be sufficient; do not assume all variants are identical.

## Send with each model

1. Original download/source link, creator name and reuse licence or permission.
2. The model files and textures. GLB or glTF is preferred; OBJ/FBX with their textures can also be assessed.
3. Any stated vehicle variant, mission, scale or reference drawings.

Separate components allow staging and cutaway animations. A single fused mesh still supports an external inspection, but may not support accurate separation. Do not buy a model just for this list before its licence and structure are checked.

## Source and implementation record

The event data and per-event citations are in `public/assets/data/rocket-development.json`. Static HTML is generated by `python tools/build-rocket-timeline.py`. Each event has a stable `model` key and a matching `data-model-key` in HTML, so a future viewer can attach a verified model without rewriting historical text. No 3D viewer or placeholder is presented as completed in this version.

Sources consulted on 25 September 2026: NASA historical and mission records; Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum for the A-4/V-2 and forced labour; SES's 2017 half-year report for SES-10; SpaceX's May 2025 Falcon Payload User's Guide for first-stage reflight. The timeline is selective, not a complete global history or a current programme-status tracker. Programme decisions, atmospheric tests, launches and landings are labelled separately. The teaching notes include interpretation; event dates and historical claims are linked to their supporting records.
