Private Aviation

Most of aviation history is told through airliners and fighters. But the aircraft that taught the largest number of people to fly, and still does, is a lot smaller and a lot less famous than either.

The Cessna 172 isn't a museum piece — it's still in production today, nearly seventy years after it first flew, and remains one of the most common aircraft used to train new pilots worldwide. Very few vehicles of any kind, in the air or on the ground, have stayed in continuous production that long.

General aviation covers everything outside the airline and military world: small training aircraft, personal planes, and business jets. Its most influential aircraft isn't fast, exotic, or even particularly powerful — it's the Cessna 172, a simple, forgiving, four-seat single-engine plane first built in 1956. More than 44,000 have been produced since, making it the most-produced aircraft of any kind in history, well ahead of any airliner or fighter.

Its appeal was never performance. It was that the 172 is easy and safe to learn on, relatively cheap to operate, and durable enough to keep flying for decades with routine maintenance. That combination made it the default trainer for flight schools worldwide, which means an outsized share of the world's pilots — commercial and private alike — first took off in one.

At the other end of general aviation is the business jet, built for speed and range rather than affordability. The Learjet 23, introduced in 1963, is generally credited as the first business jet built and sold at real production scale rather than as a one-off or a converted military design. It compressed trips that took a full day of driving or commercial connections into a couple of hours, and set the template — a small, fast, privately operated jet — that the entire business aviation industry still follows.

Between those two aircraft sits most of what general aviation actually is: light trainers built for accessibility, and business jets built for speed, with very little in between built for anything else.
