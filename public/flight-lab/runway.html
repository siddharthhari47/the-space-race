Why did that happen? Model and sources

The engine pulls. Drag and the tyres push back. As speed builds, lift takes some weight off the wheels. The remaining forward force sets the acceleration.

At the same pressure, warmer air is less dense. This model then needs more speed for lift-off and assumes less thrust. Extra mass also raises the required lift-off speed. Those are two fairly good reasons to pay attention before the runway disappears.

We use m·dv/dt = T − D − μ(W − L), with L = ½ρV²S·CL and D = ½ρV²S·CD. Pressure comes from the existing standard-atmosphere model at the chosen pressure altitude; density is p/(R·T), with absolute temperature in kelvin. No wind: true airspeed equals ground speed.

Fictional constants: wing area 16 m²; ground-run CL 0.60; CD 0.055; maximum CL 1.60; rolling coefficient 0.025; thrust = 2,400 N × (density / 1.225 kg/m³) × thrust setting, constant during each run. Lift-off target is 1.20 times calculated stall speed. At that speed we assume instantaneous rotation to the lift coefficient needed to support weight. The drawing's upward movement is a result cue, not a calculated climb.

Dry level runway; fixed coefficients, mass and density; no wind, slope, engine failures, ground-effect correction, rotation dynamics, braking or obstacle clearance. Full take-off distance includes transition and climb: this model stops at lift-off. The 900 kg minimum and 450 m runway are game objectives, not aircraft limits.
