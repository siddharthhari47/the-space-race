Why did that happen? Show me the arithmetic.

Weight is only half the argument. Where you put it matters too. Each load contributes mass × distance from the datum. Add those contributions, divide by total mass, and you have the balance point. Moving a load changes that point without changing the total mass.

Model assumptions and limits

One-dimensional mass balance: xCG = Σ(mx) / Σm, with uniform gravity. Fictional rectangular CG envelope 2.35–2.60 m at all masses, maximum 1,100 kg, payload objective 180 kg. No station capacity, lateral balance, fuel slosh, structural load or real-aircraft trim calculation.

Pitch demonstration: ΔCₘ = a × (xCG − xNP) / c × Δα, with a = 4.5 rad⁻¹, xNP = 2.80 m, c = 2 m and Δα = 5° converted to radians. Negative means an initial nose-down restoring tendency; positive means an initial nose-up tendency. Values are original teaching assumptions about an initially trimmed reference. Static stability is not a simulation of dynamic stability.
