From the first powered flight to today's small-satellite era — the same throughline the rest of this site explores, laid out in order.

The first powered, controlled, sustained flight — 12 seconds, 120 feet, and the start of practical aviation. Chuck Yeager becomes the first pilot to fly faster than the speed of sound, opening the door to supersonic and eventually orbital flight. The first artificial satellite reaches orbit, and the Space Race begins. Yuri Gagarin becomes the first human in space, completing a single orbit of the Earth. Neil Armstrong becomes the first person to walk on the Moon. The A300 takes to the skies for the first time — the start of the rivalry with Boeing that still defines commercial aviation today. The first reusable crewed spacecraft reaches orbit, beginning three decades of Shuttle missions. Launched into orbit, and starts sending back some of the clearest images ever taken of the universe. The station's first module, Zarya, reaches orbit — the start of over two decades of continuous human presence in space. Jordi Puig-Suari and Bob Twiggs define the 10cm CubeSat standard, opening spacecraft design to universities and startups. The standard invented in 1999 flies for the first time — the start of a more distributed Space Race. Atlantis lands for the final time on STS-135, ending 30 years of Shuttle missions and, for nearly a decade, crewed launches from U.S. soil. Two CubeSats hitch a ride alongside NASA's InSight lander — the first CubeSats to survive deep space. SpaceX launches astronauts to the ISS, the first crewed orbital flight by a private company.
