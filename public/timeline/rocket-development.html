Rocket & Space Shuttle development

Experiments become launch systems. Prototypes become spacecraft. Reuse becomes something to demonstrate in flight. Follow fourteen milestones—and the setbacks between them.

19262022

A selected history of early rocketry, the US–Soviet space race, NASA’s Shuttle and later launch systems. This is not a complete world history. Dates distinguish programme decisions, tests, launches and landings. Sources checked 17 October 2026.

14 milestones
  1. 192616 March 1926

    Experiment / Goddard’s Nell

    Liquid fuel leaves the ground

    Robert Goddard launches a liquid-propellant rocket in Auburn, Massachusetts. NASA records a 2.5-second flight: a small experiment, and the first flight of a liquid-fuelled rocket.

    What to notice

    The engine sits above the fuel and oxidizer tanks—an arrangement Goddard believed would improve stability.

  2. 19423 October 1942

    Flight test / A-4 / V-2

    The A-4 succeeds in flight

    The fourth A-4 test vehicle completes the programme’s first successful launch at Peenemünde. The weapon later known as the V-2 becomes a major step in large liquid-propellant rocketry.

    What to notice

    This development belongs to the history of Nazi weapons and forced labour as well as engineering. Production exploited concentration-camp prisoners; its human cost must remain part of the account.

  3. 19574 October 1957

    Orbital milestone / Sputnik 1 launch

    A satellite reaches Earth orbit

    The Soviet Union launches Sputnik 1, the first artificial Earth satellite. The achievement turns orbital flight into a demonstrated capability and accelerates the US–Soviet space race.

    What to notice

    Reaching orbit becomes a practical launch-system objective. This milestone is the satellite launch, not the start of rocket development.

    Read the source

  4. 19679 November 1967

    Integrated test / Saturn V · Apollo 4

    Saturn V flies for the first time

    Apollo 4 is the first Saturn V launch. Its objectives include rocket–spacecraft compatibility, stage separation, structural behaviour and Apollo re-entry systems. NASA reports that all mission objectives were achieved.

    What to notice

    A launch vehicle must work as an integrated system: propulsion, structures, separation and spacecraft interfaces are tested together.

    Read the source
    NASA · First launch of Saturn V ↗
    #saturn-1967
  5. 19725 January 1972

    Programme decision / Space Transportation System

    A reusable transport system is approved

    President Richard Nixon announces the decision to develop the Space Shuttle. The programme centres on a vehicle intended to make repeated journeys between Earth and orbit.

    What to notice

    The eventual system combines an orbiter, an external tank and two solid rocket boosters. It is partially reusable: the external tank is not recovered for another flight.

    Read the source
    NASA · The Space Shuttle, overview and programme decision ↗
    #shuttle-1972
  6. 197712 August 1977

    Atmospheric test / Enterprise · Approach and Landing Tests

    Enterprise flies the Approach and Landing Tests

    Enterprise makes its first independent flight during the Approach and Landing Tests. Released from its carrier aircraft, the prototype tests the Shuttle’s approach and landing behaviour before orbital flights begin.

    What to notice

    Approach and landing were tested in the atmosphere before the Shuttle flew in orbit. Enterprise never flew in space.

    Read the source

  7. 198112 April 1981

    First orbital flight / Columbia · STS-1

    Columbia takes the Shuttle into space

    John Young and Robert Crippen launch aboard Columbia on STS-1, the first orbital Space Shuttle mission. The flight brings the programme from atmospheric tests to a crewed orbital demonstration.

    What to notice

    The orbiter’s runway landing is one part of a system that must also survive launch, orbital operations and atmospheric entry.

    Read the source

  8. 198628 January 1986

    Crew loss / Challenger · STS-51L

    Challenger and its crew are lost

    Challenger is lost 73 seconds into flight with all seven crew members. The event becomes a defining break in the Shuttle programme’s history.

    What to notice

    A development timeline must include failures and their consequences, not only successful launches. Follow NASA’s history collection for the investigation record.

    Read the source
    NASA History · Challenger resources ↗

  9. 198829 September 1988

    Return to flight / Discovery · STS-26

    Discovery returns the fleet to flight

    Discovery launches on STS-26, the first Shuttle flight after Challenger. NASA’s mission history documents the preparation and return to flight.

    What to notice

    Returning to flight is a separate development milestone, rather than a continuation that erases the preceding accident.

    Read the source

  10. 20031 February 2003

    Crew loss / Columbia · STS-107

    Columbia is lost during re-entry

    Columbia and its seven crew members are lost during atmospheric re-entry, shortly before the scheduled landing. NASA’s history collection links the accident investigation and supporting records.

    What to notice

    Safe orbital operations alone do not establish a safe mission. The return through the atmosphere remains part of the vehicle’s complete flight system.

    Read the source
    NASA History · Columbia resources ↗
    #columbia-2003
  11. 200526 July 2005

    Return to flight / Discovery · STS-114

    Discovery flies the second return-to-flight mission

    Discovery carries the STS-114 crew on the Shuttle programme’s return to flight after Columbia. This is Discovery’s second return-to-flight role, following STS-26 in 1988.

    What to notice

    Discovery links two distinct recovery periods in the programme: the return after Challenger in 1988 and the return after Columbia in 2005.

    Read the source

  12. 201121 July 2011

    Final landing / Atlantis · STS-135

    Atlantis closes the Shuttle era

    Atlantis lands at Kennedy Space Center to complete STS-135. NASA’s Shuttle fleet ends a programme of 135 missions spanning 30 years of orbital flight.

    What to notice

    The Shuttle carried crews and cargo, supported satellite work and helped build the International Space Station. This date marks the final landing, not the final launch.

    Read the source
    NASA · Space Shuttle programme overview ↗
    #atlantis-2011
  13. 201730 March 2017

    Booster reflight / Falcon 9 · SES-10

    Falcon 9 launches SES-10

    SES-10 launches on a Falcon 9 on 30 March 2017, a date SpaceX lists on its mission page. SES says it became the first geostationary satellite launched on a flight-proven first-stage booster, and SpaceX’s user guide dates the first reflight of an orbital-class rocket to 2017.

    What to notice

    The milestone is reflight of the first-stage booster, not reuse of the entire launch vehicle. Recovery and reflight are different achievements.

  14. 202216 November 2022

    Integrated flight test / SLS · Artemis I

    SLS and Orion fly Artemis I

    Artemis I launches the Space Launch System and uncrewed Orion spacecraft. NASA describes it as the first integrated flight test of SLS, Orion and Exploration Ground Systems.

    What to notice

    This milestone tests a deep-space exploration system. It is a completed historical flight, not a forecast of later Artemis missions.
