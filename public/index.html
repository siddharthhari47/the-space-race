Interactive aerospace

The Space
Race

Everything that flies sits somewhere on one axis. Below about twenty kilometres there is air to push against, so you build a wing. Above a hundred there is nothing to push at all, so you carry your own reaction mass and go sideways fast enough to keep missing the ground.

One variable, two wings. Pick one.

06

About me

The short version is below. The long one is on its own page.

Hi, I'm Sid.

I'm an Electronics and Communication Engineering student at BITS Pilani, and I'm a little obsessed with figuring out how things work.

It started at a Boeing plant, sometime in middle school. I don't think I understood the scale of what I was looking at. I just remember that the building was absurd, the aircraft inside it were somehow more absurd, and I did not shut up about it for weeks afterwards. My dad bought me a model 747-8 on that trip, and it did not stay a model for very long. I started modifying it with clay — solar panels along the top of the wings, because the sun was right there, so why wouldn't you use it — and I had an entire list of improvements that Boeing had apparently overlooked.

Nobody had explained mass budgets to me, or drag, or what actually happens to a control surface when you press a slab of clay onto it. I understand a lot more about why most of those ideas wouldn't work now. The instinct hasn't changed, though. I still look at a machine and immediately start wondering what I'd do differently. The only real difference is that now I have to check.

Which is roughly what this site is for. Learning aerospace mostly means reading, and then more reading, until you have seventeen tabs open and a headache. I wanted to find out whether you could interact with the thing instead — rotate the aircraft, change the orbit, build the CubeSat, and see what breaks. It's part learning resource, part experiment, and part me seeing how far a browser will go.

If you leave here knowing every equation behind orbital mechanics, I've probably failed a little. I'd rather you left with a question.
