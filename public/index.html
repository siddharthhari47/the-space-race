The Space
Race

Everything that flies sits somewhere on one axis. Below about twenty kilometres there is air to push against, so you build a wing. Above a hundred there is nothing to push at all, so you carry your own reaction mass and go sideways fast enough to keep missing the ground.

One variable, two wings. Pick one.

01 100 km and above Space Nothing to push against, and nothing to breathe. Orbit turns out to be a sideways problem, not a height problem. Enter → 02 0 – 20 km Aircraft Air to work with. Everything in this wing is an argument about how to use it, and what happens as it thins out. Enter →

Behind this text The curve on the left is air density, ρ(h) = ρ₀·e−h/H, scale height H ≈ 8.5 km, plotted as width against a logarithmic altitude axis. It is the reason the airliner cruises where it does and the reason the station does not.
