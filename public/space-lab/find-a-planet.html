Twenty days of another star the size of the Sun. This planet crosses several times, once each orbit. Mark two dips that come one after the other.

Level 3: a planet the size of Earth

400 days of a third Sun-size star. An Earth-size planet crosses it every 12.3 days, at each blue tick. Each crossing dims the star by 84 parts per million; each reading scatters by about 250 parts per million. One dip is lost in the noise.

Stacking the transits

Each reading is moved to its time from the nearest crossing, so the crossings pile up on top of each other. The blue line is the average for each three hours.

What you found

Jupiter crossing the Sun

Earth crossing the Sun

The depth gives the size. The time from one dip to the next gives the length of the planet's year, as long as the two dips are neighbours: two dips with another between them are two orbits apart. A planet with a long year may cross only once while the telescope is watching, as in level 1, and then the year stays unknown.

A small planet's dip can be smaller than the scatter in the readings, so one crossing on its own shows nothing. Stacking many crossings averages the noise down: the scatter in an average of N readings falls as one over the square root of N, so 33 crossings cut it almost six times while the dip stays the same depth. It only works at the right period. At a wrong one the crossings land in different places and cancel out.

TRAPPIST-1 e is about the size of Earth, but its dip is 0.5%, sixty times Earth's across the Sun. Its star is only about an eighth of the Sun's width, so the same planet covers far more of it. Kepler-22 b is twice Earth's width around a star a little smaller than the Sun, and its dip is about 0.05%.

NASA's Kepler telescope, launched on 6 March 2009, watched about 150,000 stars for dips like these and found more than 2,600 planets before it was retired on 30 October 2018. Most of the planets known around other stars were found this way.

How deep the dip is tells you how big the planet is. How often it comes back tells you how long its year is.
