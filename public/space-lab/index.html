Orbits, actually to scale

Nearly every diagram of Earth orbit exaggerates the heights, which is why people picture the space station as being somewhere near the Moon. This one does not. Set an altitude and watch what it costs you.

Orbit Low Earth orbit

Drag still matters here. Uncorrected, orbits below roughly 400 km decay within years.

Scale Earth and every orbit radius drawn to one linear scale. Satellite dots are not. Motion true relative angular rates, time-compressed equally

Watch it for a few seconds and Kepler's third law does the explaining. The station laps the planet while geostationary barely moves, because period grows as r3/2 — and the further out you go, the slower you travel as well as the further you have to go.

Both numbers come from the same place. Orbital speed is v = √(μ/r) and period is T = 2π√(r³/μ), with μ = 398,600 km³/s² for Earth. Everything on that panel is those two lines of arithmetic, which is why the readouts agree with a reference book at every altitude and not just the famous ones.
